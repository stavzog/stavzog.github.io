~*~ Decoupling a legacy pricing system ~*~

← Back

At Infiterra, a product's price was being resolved along two separate paths. I mapped out the legacy system, designed a dedicated pricing engine, and built a runnable proof of concept for an auditable calculation path.


  


  
THE PROBLEM

  
STOREFRONT  ──>  legacy store procedures  ──>  finished price
CHARGING    ──>  separate rule replay ──>  re-derived price

  
The storefront and charging paths read overlapping pricing data but did not resolve the same full set of rules. As a result, pricing logic had more than one home, and the two paths could drift.


  
MY CONTRIBUTION

  
Under the guidance of an Infiterra senior architect, I worked on defining a dedicated pricing service.

  
    
    
  • Traced the legacy resolution flow across SQL, charging, and the order boundary.
    • 
    
  • Designed a dedicated pricing service with explicit data and calculation boundaries.
    • 
    
  • Implemented a runnable prototype for the math engine, quantity-tier pipeline, and per-segment audit trail.
    • 
  

  
These efforts led to the publishing of a Request for Comment (RFC) jira ticket. The next step is to write an Architecture Decision Record (ADR).


  
THE PROPOSED FLOW

  
Resolve against the pricing context itself, decoupled from the order item. Then return one result that the rest of the platform can consume.

  
(product, quantity, customer, currency, date)
        │
        ├─ resolve baseline cost candidates
        ├─ collect ordered modifiers and quantity scopes
        ├─ slice quantity into affected segments; apply pricing steps
        └─ return resolved price + segment-by-segment explanation
  


  

  

    One small math model for many pricing rules
    
The prototype represents a pricing operation as clamp(m × x + b, lo, hi). Setting, scaling, adding, and applying a floor become data in a PriceStep and there is no separate branches in the calculation loop.

  

  

    Quantity tiers are slices, not a whole-order switch
    
The engine splits the requested quantity at rule boundaries. A tier's modifier applies only to its matching units, so a discount for units 6–10 does not leak onto units 1–5 or 11 onward.

  

  

    Keep data access out of the calculation
    
Candidate adapters find the baseline; modifier adapters emit ordered, quantity-scoped steps. The engine receives those inputs in process and returns a resolved price with a separate audit trail for each quantity segment.
